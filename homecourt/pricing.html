HomeCourt Pricing: Compare the Plan You Actually See

Compare the price on your checkout screen with the practice you will actually do. No assumed subscription price required.

The quick answer

The US App Store listing reviewed on November 11, 2026 lists HomeCourt Monthly at $7.99 and HomeCourt Yearly at $69.99, alongside a free download with in-app purchases. These are public listing prices, not a quote for your account. Confirm the offer and included features at checkout, then use your own numbers in the calculator.

What the US listing shows

At the listed monthly price, twelve payments total $95.88; the listed yearly purchase is $69.99. That arithmetic alone does not decide which plan suits you. A short season, a different regional offer or features you do not need can change the decision. The listing also contains older purchase names, so use the actual offer presented to your account rather than selecting a price from a historical SKU.

Separate a free download from the feature you need

List the specific feature you intend to use: basic recording, older workout history, a training program or household access. Check whether that feature is available in your account before starting a subscription. Download availability and premium entitlement are different questions. Save the offer details you are comparing so a later renewal is not confused with an introductory offer.

Compare equal billing periods

An annual price should be compared with twelve monthly payments only if you expect to keep using the service for a year. A lower annual equivalent does not automatically mean a lower total cost for a short season. Enter the price and billing period you actually see, then estimate the number of sessions you are realistically likely to complete.

Check the whole household or team arrangement

Do not divide a plan price by a roster size until you confirm the allowed profiles and who receives the benefits. Individual, family and team arrangements can have different conditions. Record the organizer, purchase channel and renewal date along with the amount. A player leaving the team is a separate event from changing the subscription.

Match spending to the outcome

If you only need a manual shooting record, test the free browser shot chart first. If your goal is basketball editing, evaluate the clipping workflow separately. Spending more on a training plan does not itself produce a finished video, and paying for editing does not imply access to HomeCourt training history.
