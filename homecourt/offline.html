Using HomeCourt Offline: Recording, Upload & Video Checks

A court with weak reception calls for a plan before practice and a verification step afterwards.

Use the checklist ↓

By Hoopcut · Reviewed Nov 11, 2026
Independent guide · Official references below

The quick answer

HomeCourt documents offline activity recording, followed by reconnecting to Wi-Fi with the app open for upload. It also lists features that require internet or may not update correctly for offline sessions. Offline recording should not be assumed to preserve every online feature.

Prepare while you still have a connection

Open the app and make sure the intended account and activity are available before heading to the court. Confirm enough device storage and battery for your plan. Record and inspect a short test on your own device rather than assuming that a previous online session proves the offline path works the same way.

Keep a modest independent record

Write down the session time and the activity you completed. If the record matters for a team assignment, agree in advance how to report an upload delay. Avoid repeatedly starting new sessions simply because an online result has not appeared. A manual attempt log is useful context, but it cannot recreate a video that failed to save.

Verify upload after reconnecting

Follow the official guidance to reconnect and open HomeCourt in the foreground. Then inspect the activity and try its video. Do not stop at a thumbnail or a total shot count. Note which outputs are actually present and which remain unavailable before deciding that the session is safely retained.

Treat leaderboards and sharing as separate checks

The official offline article warns that some records and standings may not update even after a later upload. If participation depends on an online result, check the requirements before the session. For sharing a recap, confirm a playable exported file rather than assuming the workout’s appearance in a list makes it available to an editor.
