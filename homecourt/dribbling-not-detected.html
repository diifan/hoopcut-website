HomeCourt Dribbling Not Detected: Setup Checks

A dribbling drill needs a different view from a shot at the basket. Check your movement space before trying again.

Use the checklist ↓

By Hoopcut · Reviewed Nov 11, 2026
Independent guide · Official references below

The quick answer

HomeCourt’s movement guidance asks for a clear view of the whole body, visible ground in front of the player and hands that are not hidden by the body. Start with the drill’s on-screen setup instructions, then use a short controlled test.

Frame the movement rather than the basket

Set up for the area in which the player will move. A distant half-court shooting view is not automatically suitable for an activity that reads body movement. Check the screen with the player at the left, right and nearest edges of the intended space. If part of the body disappears, adjust the view before beginning.

Remove avoidable visual ambiguity

Inspect the background and the contrast between the player, ball and surroundings. Keep other people out of the test area and use consistent lighting. Follow the stance shown for the specific drill rather than inventing a different starting pose. A short test helps distinguish a setup issue from confusion about the activity instructions.

Compare the same movement after one change

Choose one short movement sequence and repeat it after a single setup change. Note the phone angle, position and whether hands or ball left the frame. A new drill, a new person and a new camera position all at once make it hard to identify what changed. Keep the comparison descriptive; do not turn a few attempts into an accuracy claim.

Keep editing separate from movement detection

If you film the practice for a recap, store that recording separately from any drill score. Use official HomeCourt support for a persistent activity-specific error.
