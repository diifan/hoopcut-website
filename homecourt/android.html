HomeCourt on Android: Availability & Practical Options

Have an Android phone? Separate the app you can install from the basketball task you can still complete.

Use the checklist ↓

By Hoopcut · Reviewed Nov 11, 2026
Independent guide · Official references below

The quick answer

HomeCourt’s public help page says it is available on iOS only. Hoopcut’s released app is also for iPhone. For manual basketball shot recording on an Android browser, the Hoopcut shot chart is an option; it does not provide camera-based tracking.

Start with the official distribution source

Check the official availability link below rather than treating an unrelated app with a similar name as HomeCourt. This guide does not provide an APK or claim that an iPhone-only app runs on Android. An unofficial download is not evidence of compatibility, account access or equivalent features.

Record a session without installing a training app

For a manual practice record, open the shot chart in your phone browser. Choose Made or Missed and tap the approximate location. Enter all attempts, including misses, then download the record before leaving. You supply the observations; no camera is identifying the ball or deciding whether a shot went in.

Keep a usable video alongside the log

A normal camera recording and a separate attempt log can serve different purposes. Before practice, record a short test and replay it to confirm that the player and basket stay in frame. Use timestamps to connect selected attempts to the source video. Do not assume the browser shot chart imports, analyses or synchronises that recording.

Evaluate automatic Android tracking separately

If automatic counting is essential, make confirmed Android availability a requirement when comparing other products. Check the official store listing for your device and region, then test an actual session before relying on its numbers. A browser log can be a fallback, but it should not be advertised as equivalent to automatic tracking.
