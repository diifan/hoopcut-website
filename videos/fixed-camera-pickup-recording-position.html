Play notes by Hoopcut · Updated November 6, 2026

Check the full shot corridorKeep the camera outside active playReview a test shot on the deviceQuestions
01

Check the full shot corridor

Before recording, inspect the image from likely shooting areas to the rim. Leave space above the basket for the ball’s arc and below it for the players’ feet. A court-wide view offers context, but a ball that becomes only a few pixels wide may be difficult to follow. Test the actual camera position rather than choosing it from a diagram alone.

02

Keep the camera outside active play

Use a stable position that does not obstruct players or spectators. The example shows how one stationary view can retain several players during a shot. It is not a universal mounting location: court boundaries, barriers and available space differ. Follow the venue’s rules and obtain permission where required.

03

Review a test shot on the device

Record a short sample, then replay it at the size you expect viewers to use. Confirm that the ball, rim and shooting motion are readable. Check for bodies repeatedly blocking the basket and for bright lights washing out the ball. Reposition before a full session if the critical action is consistently hidden.

Questions about this basketball clip

Is the widest possible view always best?

No. Extra context is useful only if the ball and basket remain readable.

Does this example prove a universal camera height?

No. It illustrates a fixed view, not a measured camera-installation specification.
