01

Inspect the real moving ball

Look at the ball against several backgrounds: the wall, backboard and brighter ceiling area. A trail may draw attention to a ball that is otherwise small, but it cannot restore details that were never recorded. Check the original footage first so the graphic does not disguise a weak source.

02

Look for changes across the frame

Indoor lights can produce bright regions, glare or inconsistent exposure. Watch a complete shot rather than judging only a still image near the rim. If the ball becomes difficult to distinguish during part of the flight, mark that interval for closer review. Do not assume the entire recording is unusable because one background is challenging.

03

Choose an edit that remains readable

Prefer a continuous segment where release, flight and finish can be followed. Avoid increasing sharpness or contrast until the image looks artificial merely to make a ball stand out. Compare the edited clip on a phone and a larger display, including the original creator’s visible branding.

Questions about this basketball clip

Does a bright gym guarantee a clear ball trail?

No. Motion blur, contrast and distance also affect the recorded ball.

Can the overlay recover an obscured ball?

It should not be treated as evidence for positions hidden from the camera.
