How to Make a Basketball Highlight Video: From Game Footage to Reel

Choose the right plays, keep the action clear and build a basketball highlight reel. Includes a sample clip log and an export checklist.

By Hoopcut · October 23, 2026

To make a basketball highlight video, start with one game recording, choose the player and audience, select complete plays, arrange the strongest moments, then export and review the result. The most useful first edit is one a viewer can follow without you explaining who has the ball. Here is a practical workflow you can adapt to your footage.

Choose the audience before choosing clips

A social reel can focus on a memorable run, a great finish or a shared team moment. A family keepsake can include reactions and celebrations. A video for a coach should follow that recipient’s instructions and preserve the decisions around each play. Write one sentence describing the job of your video before opening the editor. Keep separate versions if you need to serve different audiences.

Start with one recording and identify the player

Keep the original recording and work from a copy. Note the game, jersey color and number so you can check who appears in each clip. Begin with footage where the ball and hoop are visible. If players overlap or the camera misses the finish, review nearby frames before deciding whether a moment belongs in the reel. Avoid guessing the result of an obscured play.

Build a shortlist of complete plays

Mark the source time and the reason to keep each possession. Include passes, defensive reads or off-ball movement when they matter to the story, rather than filling every slot with another basket. Hoopcut’s product overview describes importing a recording, using AI to find moments, choosing a player and reviewing their plays. Review the suggestions against your source; the page does not establish which play types are detected in every recording.

Trim around the decision and its outcome

Start early enough to see the catch, pass or movement that creates the chance. End after the immediate result is clear. For an assisted basket, a cut beginning at the release may hide the contribution of the passer. Try a slightly longer version before adding explanatory text. Remove a repeated possession before sacrificing the context that makes a different one understandable.

Arrange a short first cut

Open with a clear, representative play. Follow with moments that add something new: a different finish, a passing decision or a team sequence. Finish on a deliberate ending instead of leaving the recording running. For a social editing exercise, try four to six clips and adjust from there; this is a planning suggestion, not an app limit or recruiting requirement. Watch the sequence without music to check whether it holds together.

Use effects only where they help

Hoopcut describes combining selected clips and adding Shot Tracer to show the ball’s flight. Compare the effect with the clean play and make sure the ball remains readable. Keep a clean version when the audience needs to evaluate the action. Titles, voice messages, soundtracks and other finishing steps in this guide are general editing choices; use a suitable editor for any controls unavailable in your current build.

Export, reopen and check the actual file

Watch the exported file outside the editor. Check the first frame, every clip boundary and the ending. On a phone, confirm that the player and ball remain visible. If the video will play on a school screen, test that screen’s playback setup as well. Keep the full recording and a clean master so you can revise the edit without starting again from a compressed social upload.

Before you export

  • The viewer can identify the player before the key action.
  • Each clip shows enough setup and a visible outcome.
  • Repeated clips have been removed.
  • The exported file plays correctly on the intended device.

Practical questions

How long should the video be?

Choose the audience first. A short social recap and a coach’s evaluation video have different jobs. Follow any recipient instructions, then remove clips that add no new information.
