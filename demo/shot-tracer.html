THE EFFECT

What does a basketball Shot Tracer show?

A basketball Shot Tracer adds a visible path to a recorded shot, helping a viewer follow the ball’s flight. The effect is most useful when it stays aligned with the real action and leaves the player, ball and basket easy to see. It is a visual aid rather than a substitute for the underlying footage.

This demo uses actual sample video with the trajectories already rendered into the picture. Choose between three prepared plays and inspect each one using replay, the timeline or half-speed playback. The page does not track a new ball, generate a new trajectory or edit the existing effect.

For a step-by-step review workflow, read how to add and review a basketball shot trajectory. The controls above let you inspect the prepared output directly instead of relying on a still image of the effect.

TRY IT YOURSELF

How to use this interactive demo

  1. Choose a prepared play

    Select Blue #1, Red shorts or Black kit to jump to its sample shot. Each option points to a different segment of the existing reel. The selected card and shot label tell you which play is active; replay starts from that segment’s beginning.

  2. Inspect the action within the clip

    Use Set-up, Mid-play and Finish to pause at different positions inside the chosen segment. These are fixed positions within the clip, not automatically detected release, apex or rim-contact events. Move the timeline yourself when you want to inspect a more precise frame.

  3. Replay at half speed

    Choose Replay at 0.5× to watch the selected shot more slowly. This changes playback speed only; it does not retrack the ball or change the rendered trajectory. Use the speed control to return to normal playback and judge how the effect reads at its intended pace.

  4. Check the result against the ball

    Compare the visible line with the recorded ball and basket. Look for the effect drifting away from the shot, obscuring important action or remaining visible too long. For your own footage, inspect the actual saved output before sharing it outside the editing app.

BEFORE YOU SHARE

What to check in your basketball footage

Practical review checklist
CheckWhat to look for
Release and early flightThe ball should remain understandable as it leaves the player. An effect should not hide the shooting hand or add a misleading starting point.
Path through the frameWatch for drift when the camera moves or another player blocks the ball. A pleasing line is not evidence that it follows the recorded shot accurately.
Basket and outcomeKeep the rim and result visible. A trajectory effect should not be treated as independent proof that the shot went in.
Normal-speed viewingAfter slowing down to inspect details, replay at normal speed. The line should help a viewer follow the action without overwhelming it.

GOOD TO KNOW

Common questions

Can I turn the trajectory off to compare the original?

Not with the current sample. Its trajectory is already embedded in the video, and a matching untraced source is not included. The demo therefore does not offer a before-and-after switch that would misrepresent the footage.

Can I change the tracer color, thickness or path here?

No. This page provides playback and inspection controls for pre-rendered footage. Those controls do not modify the existing line, and they do not demonstrate that any particular styling control is available in the app.

Can I upload my own basketball shot?

Not on this demo page. Use Hoopcut on iPhone for your own footage, and check the current build for access and supported options. This page does not upload or process a personal video.

Does a Shot Tracer measure shot accuracy or ball speed?

This demo does not provide either measurement. It shows a visual trajectory in recorded footage, not a calibrated measurement system or a certified scoring result.

Why should I review the effect in slow motion?

Slower playback gives you more time to compare the line with the actual ball. It is useful for inspecting alignment, but it does not improve the underlying tracking. Always check how the result looks at normal speed too.
