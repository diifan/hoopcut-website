Full-court shot scoring under NBA playing rules

NBA Rule 5 values a legal made field goal outside the offensive three-point line at three points. The distance beyond that line does not create a four-point or five-point basket. A long attempt still has to be legal and count as a field goal. A special exhibition or local shooting contest may set different point values, so identify the rule set before labeling the clip.

Keep the release and result in your recording

A long flight may move far beyond a camera view chosen only for the shooter. Check that your view includes the release, ball flight and far basket. Keep the full original sequence. If the video is being used to discuss a live-game call, retain any available clock and surrounding action as well.

Separate what is visible from what you infer

Write down which details the recording actually shows. If the release, foot position or clock is out of view, preserve that uncertainty in your description rather than treating the edited highlight as proof of the missing detail.

Full-court scoring and video questions

Is a made full-court shot worth four points in the NBA?

No. A legal made field goal from that distance is worth three points under NBA Rule 5.

Does this answer cover every basketball competition?

This page states the NBA rule. Check the rules for your own league, exhibition or shooting challenge before applying a point value.

Can the tracer confirm whether the basket should count?

No. It adds a visual trail. It does not officiate timing, foot position, violations or scoring.

What if the ball disappears halfway through the clip?

Try a clearer source angle or another attempt. A tracer cannot recover a reliable view of a ball that is missing from the recording.

Can I try the effect on a shorter shot?

Yes. Upload your own clear basketball recording, choose up to 30 seconds and preview or export Firework free.
