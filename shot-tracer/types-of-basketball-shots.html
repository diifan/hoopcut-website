Describe types of basketball shots using visible details

Choose a simple description that matches your recording, such as a jumper, layup or free throw. Keep the beginning of the action visible so viewers can understand the label without guessing from the final frame.

Avoid labels the clip cannot support

If the recording hides the approach or release, use a broader description. A short excerpt may not show enough to explain the entire play.

Compare your shot example with its traced version

Select up to 30 seconds with the ball and hoop visible. After marking the hoop, watch the preview alongside your memory of the unedited recording. Check that the trail follows the visible flight.

Treat the trail as an illustration

The effect draws attention to movement. It does not provide a shot classification, shooting score or an assessment of your form.

Organize different shot clips for later viewing

Save each original and traced copy together. Include a descriptive filename, recording date and source timestamp so you can find the practice moment again.

Use one clear example at a time

A focused clip is easier to explain than several unrelated attempts. Leave enough time after the release to show what actually happened.

Basketball shot types and tracer questions

Does HoopCut recognize types of basketball shots?

No. You choose the description yourself. This page adds a visual ball trail rather than automatically classifying the shot.

Can I use a layup or free-throw clip?

Choose a recording where the ball and hoop remain visible, then inspect the preview. The result depends on the footage and the selected range.

Will the tracer tell me whether my technique is correct?

No. The trail is a visual effect, not a coaching assessment, technique score or measurement of shooting performance.

Can I save a traced shot example free?

Yes. Export the Firework effect from the browser workflow. Keep the original recording so you can also show the play without effects.
