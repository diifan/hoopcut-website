Organize your basketball footage before editing

Save the unedited recording alongside any short version you make. Note the recording date, the player shown and the excerpt's position in the original. Use descriptive filenames so a practice shot, game sequence and edited highlight remain easy to tell apart. Keep a short note about the session with the files, adding only details you can verify.

Identify the player and original sequence

Use a consistent player name or identifier across your files. Note the source timestamp so you can quickly locate the possessions surrounding an edited shot.

NBA scouts and basketball video questions

What does an NBA scout do?

An NBA scout evaluates players using game observation, video and background research. The linked Wizards article describes this work.

Does HoopCut submit or evaluate my video for scouting?

No. HoopCut adds a visual tracer; it does not contact scouts, submit to programs or assess a player's ability.

What details should I save with a clip?

Keep the recording date, player identifier and start and end times within the source video. Use the original file to check any details you add.

Should I keep a copy without effects?

Yes. Keeping the original makes it possible to view the recording without the added trail and recover the surrounding sequence.

What can I try on this page?
