Check what your basketball game recording app actually saves

Start with the file you need at the end: a playable recording that you can locate, copy and edit. Check the recorder’s supported device, storage location and export options before the game. Apple’s Camera guide explains the video controls on iPhone; recording options vary by model. Test the settings you plan to use in the same gym, then play the saved test rather than relying only on the live camera view.

Frame the sequence you will want to show

A close view of the shooter can lose the ball at the top of its flight. Leave room above the basket and enough court around the shooter for the attempt to make sense. Keep the camera steady when possible, and check that spectators or equipment will not block the view.

Make a short capture-and-playback check

Record an ordinary attempt, stop recording and replay it. Confirm that the player remains identifiable, the ball is visible in flight and the finish stays in frame before committing to a longer recording.

  1. Confirm available storage and battery before recording.
  2. Keep a note of the period or time when an interesting play occurs.
  3. Save the complete recording before making edited highlights.

Turn a saved shot into a tracer clip

Find the sequence in your game file and upload the recording to HoopCut. Choose up to 30 seconds with a little context before release and after the result. If the ball leaves the image, choose another angle or attempt from your footage. Preview the trace before downloading Firework.

Keep game time and file time separate

A scoreboard time and a video timestamp may describe different points. Write both down when available so you can locate the play in the original file after you have exported a shorter version.

Basketball recording and tracer questions

Does this page record a live basketball game?

This page imports a video already saved on your device. Use your camera or recording app to capture the game first.

Can I upload an entire game?

The upload limit is 512 MB, and each web result covers up to 30 seconds. Prepare a smaller source excerpt if the game file exceeds the upload limit.

Should I zoom in on the shooter?

Check that your framing still contains the ball’s complete flight and the basket. Choose the view that preserves the sequence you want to show.

Does the recorder need to be HoopCut?

You can start with your own saved basketball footage. The web upload accepts MP4, MOV and WebM files; preview the result to confirm your file works.

Will a tracer fix a blocked recording?

The visual effect cannot restore action that was hidden or outside the frame. Try a clearer recording or select another attempt.
